Session 4 · 18 September 2026 · Chapter 3 — The Long Road to Gracklstugh

The Shores of Darklake

Going back into a town with a demon lord still in it, a bandit ambush that finally puts clothes on their backs, and three of the party discovering they can do things they could not do before.

Sloobludop is still coming apart behind them, and it will be a while before anyone in the party does anything sensible.

Counting heads

Two miles out, in the dark, they stop and take stock. Derendil is not with them. Ilyrion doubles back along the path they ran down, shouting his name in Elvish into the noise, and gets nothing. The quaggoth who tore a spectator apart for them is simply gone.

The prisoner they cut loose at the altar gives his name as Hemeth — a duergar, he says, an arms dealer from Gracklstugh who was trading at Sloobludop when the kuo-toa seized him. He wants to go home.

Home, it turns out, is where everyone is going. Gracklstugh is three or four weeks east along the shore of the Darklake, Buppido is from there too, and Fergus reckons the Lost Tomb of Kaine lies roughly halfway along the route.

Back into the town

In the morning, over the strenuous objections of Buppido — are you mad? — the party decides to return to the ruins. The stated reasons are Derendil, Shuushar’s body, and burial. The actual reasons are clothes and loot.

Buppido, Hemeth, Sarith and Fergus decline entirely and set off east. The party goes back alone with Stool.

They creep to the outskirts and roll a good perception check, which tells them the footsteps are faint and far off. One of them points out, correctly, that faint is a relative term where something thirty feet tall is concerned.

The first house they reach is flattened rubble. Then a roar comes out of the dark, and Demogorgon is on them.

They roll initiative at disadvantage. Jebediah meets its beguiling gaze and is stunned where he stands, and has to be physically dragged away by his own party while the rest of them run. It pursues to the edge of the town, loses interest, and goes back to destroying what’s left.

They come away with nothing at all.

Two things stay with them. Ilyrion, reaching out towards the thing’s fiendish presence, feels something horribly familiar — and privately says nothing about it. And a village being flattened by something enormous is exactly what happened at Triboar, which is why he came down here in the first place.

The bandits

They catch up with the others and push east at a hard pace, and a day later six men come over a pile of rocks at them with swords, shouting get the fuckers in Common.

It goes badly for the bandits. Thibaud takes one apart with the spear he was given at the ritual and pulls the captain’s head off with it at the end. What they leave behind finally changes the party’s situation:

  • six scimitars, a light crossbow, and a pistol worth 250 gold
  • five sets of leather armour and one studded leather
  • eight gemstones at ten gold apiece — about thirteen gold each once split

After seven weeks in their underwear, everyone is dressed.

Then, because the drow are still somewhere behind them, they strip the corpses, put their own discarded shackles on them, burn the bodies, and walk a long way through the shallows so the trail simply stops at the water.

Something is happening to three of them

The fight is also where it starts.

Mid-combat, another Rhys appears beside Rhys. The two of them look at each other, shrug, and then Rhys punches a bandit so hard the man bursts. Everyone sees the double. Nobody can explain it.

Kit swings his lead pipe, and a blue-green blade forms in his hand out of nothing. He drops it in fright and it vanishes before it lands.

Later, walking the shallows outside the range of Stool’s spores, Kit discovers he can put words directly into Thibaud’s head — one way, unasked, and only into his.

Compared against each other afterwards, the pattern is unsettling: all three of them date it to the screaming tunnel with the holes in the walls. Ilyrion points out that his own summoned sword only ever worked after he came down here.

The Weeping Steps

The shore climbs into a long terraced cliff of shallow pools brimming one into the next, lit blue by glowing mushrooms and loud with falling water. It is the first genuinely beautiful place they have been, and they spend a night bathing in it, foraging spore flour and crab, and sleeping clean.

On watch, Ilyrion tries to reach his patron properly for the first time. He finds something vast and dark that gives him no comfort at all — something like Demogorgon but not Demogorgon, and, worse, something that does not feel very far away.

Three goblins jump them on the steps the next day and are dead inside a round.

Corruption

Sarith has changed. The drow who spent every previous session clutching his skull and muttering about the pain now wants very much to go to Gracklstugh, because he likes lots of people. An arcana check finds the reason: small orange spores are growing in his skin — the same orange the party saw on the shambling hook horrors on the road to Sloobludop.

In a side cavern they meet adult myconids, who let Stool spore them all into the conversation. Stool learns Neverlight Grove lies north of Gracklstugh, and agrees to come along to the city first.

Asked about Sarith’s spores, the myconids answer through the network that connects every growing thing down here, and the answer is two words:

Corruption is spreading.

Nobody has any idea what to do about Sarith. The suggestion that a kindness might be in order is raised, and dropped.

Housekeeping and hazards

Thibaud eats a lump of green slime on the grounds that he is sick of mushrooms, discovers it is violently corrosive, throws it back up, and the party then ruins a perfectly good scimitar confirming it dissolves metal. They also pick up a tongue of madness — a mushroom that makes whoever eats it say every thought aloud for an hour — which Kit pockets, thoughtfully, near Thibaud.

The session ends at a valley full of torchstalk, the explosive fungus. Rather than walk through it they take the rope bridge strung above — and halfway across, something yellow and oozing is sliding along the ropes towards them.
