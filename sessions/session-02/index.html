Session 2 · 31 July 2025 · Chapters 1 & 2 — The Escape and Into Darkness

A Daring Escape

The break for freedom — down through the webs, past the thing in the pool, and out into the dark while demons tear the outpost apart.

The clock is running. The guard is about to change, and Jorlan has promised an unlocked gate.

A new prisoner

Before it can happen, the junior priestess Asha appears, driving a slave ahead of her with slaps and complaints — hurry up, I’m sick of looking at you, back in with the rest of this filth. She unlocks the pen, shoves her in, slams the gate, sweeps a look of contempt across the rest of them and leaves.

The newcomer is Joy: a blonde elf, stripped to her underwear like the others, her ears hacked off and scarred over as though someone had tried to make her pass for human. She backs into a corner and watches the pen warily.

Questioned in Elvish, she says she was taken on the surface. Before that she was kept in a circus, displayed in a cage — a silver piece a look. The party debates whether she is a drow plant; the consensus is that the drow would never stoop to using an elf. She earns her place by trading what she overheard while serving Asha: word of the north passage. Shuushar confirms the rest — the west passage runs towards the Darklake, and his home.

Slipping the chains

While they wait, the prisoners work at their manacles. Some are broken open by main force, others squeezed free — one pair reportedly loosened by a method best left undescribed. Those who get loose hold the chains in place so that, at a glance, nothing has changed.

Then Jorlan’s face appears at the gate without a sound of approach. He unlocks the door, pushes it open, and is gone.

The armoury they never entered

The party creeps across the rope bridge to the guard tower, everyone holding hands in the dark — the whole pen, prisoners and all. Listening at the armoury door, they hear drow inside talking and rolling dice.

What follows is the longest argument of the escape. Plans are floated and abandoned: a knock at the far door, a phantom sound to draw the guards out, Stool sporing the room so the party can whisper into drow minds, a mage hand to tip the dice and start a brawl. Against all of it stands one question, asked over and over — what is in there that is worth your lives?

They vote. The armoury is abandoned. Nobody recovers their gear, and Eldeth’s warhammer and shield stay behind in Velkynvelve.

Through the webs

Ilyrion goes first — leaping from the walkway, twisting in the air and swinging his conjured longsword through the spider silk on the way down. It is a spectacular piece of work, and it isn’t quite enough: he tears most of the web away but lands tangled in what’s left. Others follow and stick fast beside him. Two giant spiders come up over the edge of the web and begin closing.

Working in turns — tearing, hauling each other free, one hero simply ripping the last shreds apart with his hands — they open a ten-foot hole and drop through it to the pool below, spiders left behind above.

The thing in the pool

They land in the water and a voice speaks in every mind at once:

Flesh for the faceless lord.

A grey ooze rises from the pool — the creature Sarith warned them about, back when kindness bought his confidence. I told you, he says.

There is no fight worth having. Everyone swims and scrambles for the shore. The ooze is slow, and grows visibly reluctant the further it creeps from the water; it shuffles back in and the party is left standing in the dark on the cavern floor, unhurt.

The sky comes apart

Alarm horns begin sounding through the cavern. Then a horrible droning buzz.

The horns are not for them. Out of the northern passage come demons: a pair of huge, decaying, vulture-like things being chased by four bloated, mosquito-like horrors, all of them utterly unnatural even by the standards of the Underdark. They crash through the rope bridges and slam into the towers of Velkynvelve. Drow are shouting; crossbows are firing; the outpost the party spent a week enduring is coming apart above their heads.

They run west.

Freedom is in sight when one of the vulture-demons shrieks overhead and crashes to the ground directly across the western exit, badly wounded but far from dead. Its screech tears through the party — several are stunned and hurt by the sound alone. Ilyrion’s eldritch blast finds it, Jebediah calls down sacred flame, and it is Jimjar — a manacled deep gnome with no weapon at all — who springs at the thing, takes hold of its jaws and pulls until the whole ruined creature comes apart.

They climb over the corpse and run down the tunnel into the darkness of the Underdark.

They are out. Nobody has any equipment, most are still in their underwear, and behind them an enraged Ilvara is already sending soldiers out in pursuit and riding after them with her commanders.

Into darkness

The first day is a long, grim, featureless corridor — no weather, no light, no sense of time, nothing to forage. They travel fast, wanting distance more than comfort, and pay for it: by nightfall everyone is thirsty and hungry, and exhaustion begins to bite.

The tunnel finally widens onto a ledge above a cavern, lit by phosphorescent moss, with paths curving away left and right into the dark. A stone dropped over the edge falls a very long way before it stops — solid ground, not water. They post elven watches and sleep.

The traders

In the morning, four duergar — grey dwarves — come up the path from the left, driving two enormous armoured spiders laden with packs and equipment. Travelling merchants, and not remotely bothered by a ledge full of naked strangers.

Stool spores so that everyone can understand one another, and the haggling begins. The party’s entire fortune is a single flawed carnelian gem, worth ten gold, which had spent the imprisonment concealed about Thibaud’s person — a fact the merchants are gracious enough to overlook after a wash. For it they get water skins all round, a day’s rations, rope, ball bearings, two backpacks and a ten-foot pole.

The real prize is bought with words. The traders are heading to Velkynvelve to sell to the drow; the party asks them to lie. A very persuasive case is made, sweetened by a promise to put in a good word for them at Sloobludop via Shuushar, and Grimnar — the elder of the four — agrees. He will tell the drow he saw a group of filthy, penniless slaves who looked half dead, and that they went right.

The party is going left.

Mushrooms and clean water

They set off at a steadier pace so they can forage, and the Underdark is kind for once: a little grove thick with edible mushrooms, and nearby a stream lit from beneath by glowing purple fungi. Nine pounds of food and more water than they can carry.

The session closes on the morning of the second day of the flight — fed, watered, barefoot, and being hunted.
