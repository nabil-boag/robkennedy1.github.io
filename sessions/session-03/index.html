Session 3 · 22 August 2026 · Chapters 2 & 3 — Into Darkness and Sloobludop

The Deep Father

Seven days of tunnels, a favour owed to goblins, a friend lost to a spectator — and a god pulled out of the lake by a father's knife.

Seven days of walking west, and a town at the end of it that will not survive the party’s visit.

Two fewer prisoners

The morning after the traders, the deep gnome twins Topsy and Turvy are simply gone. No goodbye, no tracks — they slipped away in the night while the watch was looking elsewhere. Nobody ever did speak to them much.

The rest of the column trudges on. To slow the drow, the party takes to laying false trails at every junction: two of them walk a decoy path for half an hour, churn it up with tracks, then double back sweeping their own footprints away before taking the real route.

Company on the road

Days of nothing but grey rock give the prisoners time to talk.

Buppido turns out to be from Gracklstugh, a duergar city on the far side of the Darklake — a huge place, choked with furnace smog, and home to a red dragon that keeps the forges lit. He intends to travel with the party a while, then peel off for home. Thibaud offers to slay the dragon; Buppido advises against killing the local economy.

Shuushar paints a warier picture of his own home. Sloobludop is a trade town, safe enough and well-connected for crossing the lake, but his people are prone to madness, he is not popular there for preaching peace, and when he left there was talk of a new religion stirring up conflict.

Jimjar bets Thibaud a hundred gold that he can punch the next demon they meet. The bet is accepted.

The Underdark opens up

The tunnels finally give way to a swamp lit blue-green by glowing fungus, dotted with trees made of solid stone. It is the first beautiful thing they have seen since their capture, and it feeds them: cask-shaped fungi full of drinkable water, Nilhog’s nose for the smell of things, and torchstalk, which Sarith warns will explode if lit.

Not everything is kind. Four hook horrors — hunched, hook-armed things crusted with orange spores — shamble past the camp in the night and do not notice the watch holding very still.

Worse is a narrow single-file passage whose walls are riddled with holes. Moaning rises out of them, then shrieking and gibbering in no language anyone knows. Kit and Ilyrion both crack under it — Kit paralysed for four minutes, Ilyrion out cold for six, both left with something clinging to them afterwards.

Beyond it lies a cavern floored with fossilised bones — ribcages the size of wagons, skulls with far too many eye sockets, nothing anyone can name. Thibaud plays one of the ribcages like a xylophone, and it lifts the mood more than it has any right to.

Seven goblins find them there in the night. Kit — who grew up around goblin kids on the street and speaks the language — talks them out of a fight, insisting the party is twenty strong and boasting about all the drow they killed at Velkynvelve. The goblin leader doesn’t believe a word of it, but leaves anyway.

The Silken Paths

The route west ends at a chasm strung from wall to wall with an enormous network of spiderwebs — the Silken Paths, crossable only by crawling at half speed while something very large may or may not be watching from underneath.

Two goblins come surfing across the webs to meet them: Yuck Yuck and Spider Bait, feet slathered in grease, who make their living guiding travellers over. The party has nothing to pay with, so Thibaud settles the fee another way — a favour owed, to be named later.

Halfway across they find a cocoon that is still moving. Inside is Fergus Rumblefoot, a halfling whose companions were killed by gnolls on the way to the Lost Tomb of Kaine. Cut free and carried the rest of the way, he offers to lead the party to the tomb’s treasure — assuming they don’t mind the spiders.

The spectator, and a friend lost

Further along the webs, something floats up out of the dark: a green orb of a creature, one great eye and four more on stalks, muttering to itself and speaking into their heads. Outsiders in the underworld. Demons are rising. Thibaud, convinced it is an incubus trying to seduce him, declares he will not go to its bed.

It is a spectator, and the fight goes badly at the edges. Two spells are simply swatted back at the casters. Then the thing turns a red ray on Jimjar, who is clinging to one of its eyestalks with his bare hands, and blasts him off the web into the chasm.

Derendil tears the spectator apart a round later. It doesn’t bring Jimjar back. The gnome who killed a demon with his fists at Velkynvelve, who never stopped offering bets, is gone into the dark, and Thibaud never has to settle up.

Sloobludop

The webs give onto a bridge, the bridge onto a tunnel, and the tunnel onto the Darklake — not one lake but a chain of black water and stone columns stretching further than any light reaches. Four hours east along the shore stands Sloobludop: rickety towers lashed together with rope and plank walkways, glowing faintly in the dark.

A patrol of nine kuo-toa takes them prisoner before they reach the gates. Shuushar’s name means nothing; he is a heretic, and the party are offerings for the Deep Father.

At the gate they are claimed instead by Ploopploopeen, archpriest of the Sea Mother, who explains the town’s problem: his own daughter Bloppblippodd has raised a new cult around a god called the Deep Father, and the sacrifices are getting worse. He wants the party as false offerings — walk into his daughter’s ceremony, break their bonds, cause chaos, and give him the opening to strike her down.

They agree. Passing the Sea Mother’s shrine — a nine-foot idol with the head and claws of a giant crayfish lashed to a wooden body — Jebediah recalls that vomiting at her feet is a sincere show of peace. Two of the party promptly throw up on the goddess. Ploopploopeen is deeply moved.

The ritual

Prodded into a blood-stained depression twenty feet from the idol of the Deep Father, the party springs it all at once: Ilyrion rips out arms of Hadar, Jebediah amplifies his voice and commands the nearest guard to defecate, Stool blows out the biggest spore cloud of his life, and Thibaud — with every mind in range suddenly connected — announces himself as the Sea Mother and denounces two years of heresy.

Ploopploopeen clubs his daughter across the back of the head. His followers fall on hers. A full civil war breaks out across the docks, and monstrous manta rays, drawn by blood, begin dragging kuo-toa under the water.

Bloppblippodd, on her knees with her head bleeding, reaches out towards the lake and screams — and her father brings the staff down and kills her on the altar.

What her blood called

The lake bubbles. An oily tentacle bursts out of it, then another, and something thirty feet tall stands up out of the water: a single torso carrying two heads like enormous, furious baboons, eyes and throat burning red.

Every kuo-toa in Sloobludop — both factions, the whole town — clutches their heads and stops fighting. Rhys stares at it a moment too long and has to be dragged away babbling.

Jebediah knows it from his religious training, and wishes he didn’t. It is Demogorgon, Prince of Demons.

The first tentacle sweeps a row of buildings into the water. The second comes down on Shuushar the Awakened, who had spent the ritual watching from the crowd, sorry for what was happening to his home.

The party runs into the dark with Stool carried under one arm and a freed duergar prisoner in tow, the roars and the sound of Sloobludop being flattened following them out.

Did we just wipe out a town?

They are level 3.
