Friends, Strangers & Foes

People of the Abyss

The notable figures the party has met so far — only what the heroes themselves have learned.

Fellow Prisoners & Allies

Stool

Myconid · Fellow prisoner

A myconid the party saved from being bullied by Ront. As a result Stool is extremely friendly to them — especially Rhys and Thibaud, who stood up for him. He can release spores that let creatures within thirty feet communicate telepathically, letting the prisoners talk past any language barrier and plan their escape. Stool longs to return home to his people in Neverlight Grove, and has asked the party to come with him, promising they will be given sanctuary.

Jimjar

Deep gnome · Fellow prisoner Deceased

A male deep gnome who spoke at length with Thibaud and loves to make bets. He revealed himself as a spy from the surface city of Luruar, sent into the Underdark to investigate the status of Blingdenstone, the deep gnome city the drow drove his people out of. Friendly to the party, he proved instrumental in their escape: it was Jimjar who exposed the rift among the drow leaders — that Ilvara once kept Jorlan as her lover and second-in-command before swiftly replacing him with Shoor when Jorlan was disfigured by an ooze.

He then repaid the debt in the most unlikely way imaginable. When a wounded vulture-demon crashed down across the western exit and blocked the party’s only way out, it was Jimjar — a shackled deep gnome with no weapon at all — who leapt at the thing, took hold of its jaws and tore them apart until the whole ruined creature came down in a heap.

He never stopped offering bets. Days into the journey west he wagered Thibaud a hundred gold that he could punch the next demon they met — and then, on the Silken Paths, he grabbed an eyestalk of the spectator with his bare hands and tried to do exactly that. It turned a red ray on him, blasted him off the web, and he fell into the chasm. The bet was never settled.

Shuushar the Awakened

Kuo-toa · Fellow prisoner Deceased

A male kuo-toa, friendly and serene. He revealed that he lives a life of peace and meditation to escape the madness that befalls so many of his kind. He comforted Rhys when the rogue was distraught over his part in Ront’s death. Shuushar wished to return to his people and pass on his learnings, so that others of his kind might avoid madness too.

He guided the party west out of Velkynvelve towards his home at Sloobludop — seven days of tunnels, swamp and spiderweb — answering endless questions about the Underdark along the way, and warning them honestly that his people were erratic, that he was not popular there for preaching peace, and that a new religion had been stirring up trouble when he left.

He was right on every count. His own people took the party prisoner on the shore road and called him a heretic to his face.

He was watching sadly from the crowd when the party sprang Ploopploopeen’s trap, and he said sorry into their minds for what his home had become. Moments later Demogorgon rose out of the Darklake, and its second tentacle came down on the place where he was standing.

He never got to teach them.

Derendil

Quaggoth · Fellow prisoner Whereabouts unknown

A quaggoth who confided to Rhys that he is in fact an elven prince, ousted from his kingdom of Nelrindenvane in the High Forest by the evil wizard Terrestor — who, he claims, turned him into a quaggoth and seized his realm. He is friendly to the party.

Whatever he was once, he is formidable now. On the Silken Paths it was Derendil who closed with the spectator that had just killed Jimjar, opened with a critical hit, and a round later tore the creature apart entirely.

He did not make it out of Sloobludop. In the confusion of the escape, with Demogorgon flattening the town behind them, nobody saw where he went. Ilyrion ran back along the path calling his name in Elvish and heard nothing over the noise. The party went back into the ruins the next morning partly to look for him, and were driven off before they found anything.

No body. No trail. Just gone.

Buppido

Derro · Fellow prisoner

A male derro, and a fellow prisoner of the drow. He is friendly to the party, and endlessly chatty.

On the road west he told Thibaud that he comes from Gracklstugh, a duergar city on the far side of the Darklake — huge, dangerous, thick with furnace smog, and home to a red dragon that keeps the forges burning. He intends to travel with the party for a while and then make his own way home. He also proved useful in the fungus swamp, pointing out the cask-shaped fungi that hold drinkable water.

Sarith Kzekarit

Drow · Fellow prisoner

A male drow prisoner, unstable and seemingly not in his right mind, forever complaining of a terrible pain in his head. He was unfriendly to the party and came to blows with Thibaud, who agitated him by refusing to stop asking questions; Jebediah managed to talk him down. Sarith says he is imprisoned because he was framed for murdering another drow. After Ilyrion showed him kindness with stolen food, a calmer Sarith revealed that he knows there is an ooze in the pool at the bottom of the waterfall.

He was right. The moment the party splashed down into that pool and the grey ooze rose out of it, Sarith was there to say I told you. He escaped with them and travels with the party still — no better, muttering about killing, and nobody has yet unlocked his shackles.

On the road east from Sloobludop he changed. The clutching at his head stopped, the muttering eased, and he became suddenly and cheerfully keen to reach Gracklstugh because he likes lots of people. Looking closely, the party found small orange spores growing in his skin — the same orange that crusted the shambling hook horrors they passed on the road to Sloobludop.

Asked what it meant, the myconids answered with two words: corruption is spreading.

Nobody has told him.

Topsy and Turvy

Deep gnomes · Former fellow prisoners Whereabouts unknown

Twin deep gnomes who avoided all contact with the other prisoners.

They escaped Velkynvelve with everyone else and then slipped away in the night on the second day of the journey west, while the watch was looking elsewhere. There were no tracks to follow by the time anyone noticed, and the party was a full day past the last junction. Nobody ever did learn anything about them.

Eldeth Feldrun

Dwarf · Fellow prisoner Deceased

A female dwarf, friendly to the party. She formed a bond with Ilyrion over kitchen duty, after he suggested they help each other escape, showed he could magically create weapons, and stole food to share with her and the other prisoners. She believed that if anyone could escape, it would be him — and asked that, should she not make it, Ilyrion return her shield and warhammer to her family in Gauntlgrym.

She was murdered in her sleep during the night — strangled, a livid red mark left around her neck, her body posed kneeling as though offered up to something. Jebediah recognised it as a sacrifice to the murder god Diinkarazan, though strangely bloodless: such offerings are normally made by cutting the victim open.

Ront

Orc · Fellow prisoner Deceased

A male orc, very unfriendly to the party. He was caught bullying the myconid Stool, prompting the party to intervene. Rhys played on Ront’s vanity and persuaded him to break free of his chains in a show of strength — but the guards saw it. The drow shot Ront with poison crossbow bolts, and Ilvara had him thrown into the spider webs surrounding the outpost, where he was devoured by giant spiders. Rhys felt deep guilt over his role in the events that led to Ront’s death.

Grimnar

Duergar · Travelling merchant

The elder of four duergar — grey dwarves — met on a ledge in the Underdark on the second day of the party’s flight, driving two enormous armoured spiders loaded with packs and trade goods. Long-eared, plain-spoken, and entirely unbothered by a party of escaped slaves in their underwear.

He sold them water, rations, rope, ball bearings, two backpacks and a ten-foot pole for a single flawed gemstone, and threw in a little extra for good relations. He has no love for the drow — look what they do to us — but he trades with them all the same, and was on his way to Velkynvelve when the party met him.

Persuaded by the promise of a good word at Sloobludop, Grimnar agreed to lie to the drow: he saw some filthy, penniless slaves who looked half dead, and they went the other way.

F

Fergus Rumblefoot

Halfling · Rescued treasure hunter

A halfling the party cut out of a spider’s cocoon halfway across the Silken Paths, having been hanging there a few hours and no longer able to walk. Thibaud carried him the rest of the way, and told him repeatedly that it was Thibaud who had saved him.

He was hunting the Lost Tomb of Kaine with three companions when a pack of gnolls killed them all and the spiders took him. He is entirely undeterred: he still wants the treasure, still says he can find the way, and now wants the party to come with him — his last party, as he points out, were fully clothed and it did them no good at all.

Y

Yuck Yuck and Spider Bait

Goblins · Guides of the Silken Paths

Two goblins who make a living leading travellers across the Silken Paths, the vast spiderweb network strung over a chasm on the road to the Darklake. They practise every day, coat their feet in grease, and surf the webs at speed for the sheer pleasure of it.

Their price is two gold per person per day. The party had no money at all, so Thibaud bought passage with a favour, to be named later — a bargain the goblins seemed far more pleased with than the coin.

They were honest guides: they warned about the spiders, they let the party stop to cut a stranger out of a cocoon, and when a spectator attacked they withdrew to supervise the situation from a safe distance, exactly as promised. They melted away when the kuo-toa took the party prisoner outside Sloobludop.

H

Hemeth

Duergar · Arms dealer

The other prisoner on the altar at Sloobludop — the one who saw the party looking shifty and said if you’re up to something, get me out of here. They cut him loose in the chaos and he came out with them.

He gives his name as Hemeth, says he is an arms dealer from Gracklstugh, and that he was trading at Sloobludop when the kuo-toa took him from behind and put him up for sacrifice. He wants to go home, and has offered the party a good price on weapons when they get there.

Two of the party leaned on him about his story. He is telling the truth about the arms dealing — and he is holding something back. He and Buppido talk to each other constantly in Undercommon, which nobody else in the party speaks.

The Drow of Velkynvelve

Ilvara

Drow · Commander of Velkynvelve · Priestess

Mistress Ilvara of House Mizzrym, the commander of Velkynvelve and a drow priestess. Sadistic and cruel, she rules with a scourge in hand — fond of reminding the prisoners that their lives belong to her — and made a public example of the orc Ront, commanding that he be fed to the outpost’s giant spiders.

S

Shoor

Drow · Second-in-command

A handsome and arrogant drow, Ilvara’s second-in-command, who appears to be in a relationship with her. It was Shoor whom Ilvara ordered to throw Ront to the spiders.

J

Jorlan

Drow · Maimed warrior

A maimed drow warrior who lost his looks and his fighting skill in a fight with a black pudding (a corrosive Underdark ooze), and who bitterly resents Shoor — the rival who replaced him at Ilvara’s side. The party approached him through Stool’s telepathic spores and struck a bargain: he would let them escape, and in return they would make sure the blame fell on Shoor. Jorlan agreed — though he warned that if they betrayed him he would hunt them down and torture them for days — promising to leave the slave-pen gate open and create a distraction at the next changing of the guard. He advised the party to head first to the armoury in the guard tower, then jump into the spider webs below, make their way to the pool, and escape from there.

He kept his word. At the changing of the guard his face appeared at the gate with no sound of approach; he unlocked the door, pushed it open and vanished. The party took his route but not his advice, giving the armoury a miss. What became of him when the demons fell on Velkynvelve, nobody knows.

A

Asha

Drow · Junior priestess

A junior priestess working under Ilvara, and clearly resentful of it.

The party finally laid eyes on her on the morning of the escape, when she drove a new slave along the walkway with slaps and complaints — hurry up, I’m sick of looking at you, put you back in with the rest of this filth — unlocked the pen, shoved Joy inside, slammed the gate and swept a look of contempt over the rest of them before stalking off.

She talks more freely in front of slaves than she should. It was from serving Asha that Joy overheard what lay down the north passage.

P

Ploopploopeen

Kuo-toa · Archpriest of the Sea Mother

Archpriest of the Sea Mother at Sloobludop, and the man who claimed the party from the patrol that had taken them prisoner.

His complaint was that his own daughter, Bloppblippodd, had raised a cult around a new god — the Deep Father — and that her sacrifices were growing worse and something in the lake was feeding on them. His solution was the party: walk into her ceremony as false offerings, break their bonds, cause enough chaos to draw the guards, and give him the moment he needed.

He was truthful about the plan and generous with the details. He was also perfectly willing to bludgeon his daughter to the ground in front of her own congregation and then kill her on the altar — because if his people saw him strike her down, they would respect his strength and fear him.

He got his coup. Her blood on the altar called Demogorgon out of the lake, and Sloobludop was destroyed around him.

B

Bloppblippodd

Kuo-toa · Archpriest of the Deep Father Deceased

Daughter of Ploopploopeen, and archpriest of the Deep Father — the new god whose cult had taken hold in Sloobludop in the two years since Shuushar left it.

Her people took the party prisoner on the shore road as offerings. It was her ceremony the party were paraded into: an idol of stretched hide, dead manta ray and pinned octopuses, and a blood-stained pit twenty feet in front of it.

Her father described her as earthy, strong and cruel, and said the last thing she was in was distress. He accepted her god publicly, presented the party as his offering to her, and then clubbed her to the ground and killed her on her own altar.

As she died she reached out towards the lake and screamed. Something answered.
