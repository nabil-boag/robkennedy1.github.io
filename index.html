Out of the Abyss

A drow prison, a handful of captives, and the long climb out of the dark. This is the chronicle of our campaign — the story so far, session by session.

The Story So Far

It began in chains. Five strangers — the elf warlock Ilyrion Silverstar, the dwarf cleric Jebediah Brightstone, the human rogue Kit, the elf rogue Rhys Toromakian, and the human fighter Thibaud — woke as prisoners of the drow, stripped of everything and thrown into the slave pen of Velkynvelve. Around them languished ten other captives, all of them waiting for a slaver's party from Menzoberranzan that was already days overdue.

The outpost's commander, the priestess Ilvara, ruled by cruelty. When the party goaded the bully Ront into snapping his chains, the guards struck him down and Ilvara had him fed to the giant spiders below — a lesson written in blood. Yet even here the prisoners found one another: the party saved the gentle myconid Stool from Ront's torment, and in gratitude he gave them a priceless gift — spores that let them all speak mind to mind.

Through whispered, telepathic talk they gathered allies and secrets. The deep gnome spy Jimjar revealed a rift among the drow leaders. The serene kuo-toa Shuushar spoke of escaping madness through peace. The dwarf Eldeth placed her trust — and a dying request — in Ilyrion's hands. And the quaggoth Derendil swore he was a cursed elven prince.

Then the dark turned darker. Three of the party were visited by nightmares of groping shadows and buzzing madness, waking certain that something worse than the drow stirred in the deep. By morning Eldeth lay murdered, her body posed as a sacrifice to a god of murder. With the slavers' arrival looming, the party struck a desperate bargain with the disgraced warrior Jorlan: he would leave the gate open and raise a distraction — and they would make sure the blame fell on his rival.

The plan held. On the morning of the break-out a sixth captive was thrown in among them — the elf Joy, her ears cut away, lately the property of a circus — and then Jorlan opened the gate and disappeared. Judging the armoury not worth their lives, the party left their equipment where it lay, cut down through the giant spiders' webs and swam a pool that spoke into their minds: flesh for the faceless lord.

They surfaced into chaos. Demons came howling out of the north passage and fell on Velkynvelve, and under cover of the slaughter the prisoners ran for the west tunnel — until a dying, vulture-like demon crashed down across it, and a shackled deep gnome named Jimjar tore its jaws apart with his bare hands.

Then came seven days of tunnels with no gear, no boots and no map, an enraged Ilvara hunting them, and false trails laid at every junction. They crossed a swamp of stone trees, a cavern of nameless bones, and a chasm strung with spiderweb — where a spectator blasted Jimjar off the silk and into the dark.

At the end of the road stood Sloobludop, and it did not want them. Taken as offerings to a new god, the party were made into a distraction so that an archpriest could murder his own daughter in front of her congregation — and her blood on the altar called Demogorgon, Prince of Demons, up out of the Darklake. The town came apart behind them as they ran, with Shuushar under it.

The dreams warned of something worse than the drow. They have now seen it — and they went back the next morning anyway, for clothes, and were very nearly killed for it. Derendil never came out of that town at all.

So they walk east instead, towards the duergar city of Gracklstugh, a month away along the black shore. A band of highwaymen made the mistake of ambushing them and finally put armour on their backs. And somewhere past the screaming tunnel, three of the party began doing things they cannot explain — a man who steps out of himself, a blade of light in an empty hand, a voice inside someone else's head. The mushrooms have a phrase for what is happening down here.

Corruption is spreading.
