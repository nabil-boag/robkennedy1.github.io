The Party

Heroes

The captives whose escape from the Underdark this chronicle follows.

Ilyrion Silverstar

Elf · Warlock

A silver-haired elf warlock and a survivor of the giant attack on Triboar. Weary of being a victim, he set out in search of a patron — drawn to these caves by whispers that have since granted him his eldritch power.

On kitchen duty he won the trust of the dwarf Eldeth Feldrun, revealing that he can conjure weapons by magic once beyond the slave pen’s wards, and proposing they escape together and steal food to share with the other prisoners. He treated the troubled drow Sarith with unexpected kindness, slipping him stolen food — a gesture that calmed Sarith enough to reveal the ooze lurking in the pool beneath the waterfall. He was among those troubled by dark dreams on the final night.

Watching Demogorgon tear Sloobludop apart hit him twice over: once as the thing itself, and once as Triboar all over again — a village being flattened by something enormous, which is the reason he came down here at all.

Reaching towards its fiendish presence, he felt something familiar, and said nothing to anyone. Days later, on watch at the Weeping Steps, he tried properly to reach his patron for the first time. He found something vast and dark that offered him no comfort whatsoever — something like Demogorgon, but not Demogorgon, and something that did not feel very far away at all.

He is still keeping that to himself.

Jebediah Brightstone

Dwarf · Cleric

A devout dwarf cleric and the party’s voice of calm, sworn to Tymora, goddess of luck — forever contending with her sister Beshaba, who governs the bad sort. He defused a brawl by talking the unstable drow Sarith down with soothing words, and it was his religious knowledge that recognised Eldeth’s murdered body as an offering to the murder god Diinkarazan. He made the bargain with Jorlan that secured the party’s escape, and was one of three marked by disturbing dreams on the final night.

It was Jebediah who knew that vomiting at the Sea Mother’s feet is a sincere show of respect, and Jebediah who recognised what rose out of the Darklake afterwards. Going back into the ruins the next morning, he caught Demogorgon’s beguiling gaze full in the face and was still standing there stunned when the others took hold of him and hauled him away.

His holy symbol was taken by the drow at Velkynvelve, and without one a good half of his spells are closed to him. He carries a plain coin he intends to carve into Tymora’s likeness — her smiling face ringed with shamrocks — the moment he can lay hands on a set of jeweller’s tools.

Kit

Human · Rogue

A scruffy, scrawny human rogue — scarred and crooked-nosed from a hard-luck life. Set to work the outpost’s lift alongside Thibaud. It was Kit who turned the prisoners’ whispered gossip into a plan — proposing they exploit Jorlan’s bitterness and persuade him to let them escape, so that the blame and humiliation would fall on Shoor and Ilvara.

He grew up around goblin children on the street and speaks their language, which has twice now talked the party out of a fight in the dark.

Something changed in him after the screaming tunnel, where he collapsed. Mid-fight with a band of highwaymen he swung his lead pipe and a blue-green blade formed in his hand out of nothing — he dropped it in fright and it was gone before it hit the ground. Later, walking the lake shallows well outside the range of Stool’s spores, he found he could put words straight into Thibaud’s head. Only Thibaud’s. Only one way.

He has no explanation for either.

Rhys Toromakian

Elf · Rogue

A woodland elf rogue with a quick tongue and quicker fingers, forever anxious to get back to his father. He stood up for the myconid Stool against the bully Ront, then flattered the orc into shattering his own chains — a trick that, witnessed by the guards, led to Ront being fed to the spiders. Rhys was left wracked with guilt over his role in the orc’s death. On chamber-pot duty he picked a sleeping soldier’s chest and stole an iron bar and a vial of drow poison, and it was to Rhys that the quaggoth Derendil confided his claim to be a transformed elven prince.

Looking at Demogorgon too long at Sloobludop left him babbling, and he had to be dragged out of the town by the others.

He has not been right since the screaming tunnel, and on the road east it started showing. Struck by a bandit, Rhys watched a second Rhys step out of the air beside him. The two of them looked at each other, and then he drove his bare fist through the man hard enough to burst him. Everybody saw the double. Nobody, Rhys included, can say what it was.

Thibaud Pierre Comme Ça Jean

Human · Fighter

A vain and wealthy human fighter of House Comme Ça Jean — by his own account a charitable house and “a big name on the Sword Coast” — with a weakness for fine textiles. His first instinct in captivity was to try to bribe the drow with his family’s name and money.

He helped Rhys stand up to Ront on the myconid Stool’s behalf. His persistent questioning of the unstable drow Sarith — despite being told to back off — sparked a fistfight before Jebediah intervened. Set to work the lift, he proved too weak to turn the pulley and faked a fall onto the platform, to no avail. Through Stool’s spores he learned that the deep gnome Jimjar is in truth a surface spy.

He is in the Underdark to earn his knighthood, and is desperate for someone to write the deeds down for his father. He is accumulating some: he cut Fergus out of a spider’s cocoon and insisted the halfling remember who saved him, impersonated a goddess at the Deep Father’s altar, and killed two bandits with a borrowed spear before taking the captain’s head off with it.

He came out of the bandit fight in studded leather and carrying a pistol — a fancy newfangled thing with powder in it that nobody in the party knows how to load. He also ate a handful of green slime because he was sick of mushrooms, and had to be talked through vomiting it back up before it rotted through him.

Joy

Elf · Prisoner

A blonde elf thrown into the slave pen by the junior priestess Asha on the very morning of the escape — stripped like the rest of them, and with her ears hacked off and scarred over, as though somebody had once tried to make her pass for something other than an elf.

She says she was taken on the surface. Before that she was kept in a circus, shown off in a cage at a silver piece a look. Asked whether Joy is her real name — something that provides hope — she is unimpressed by the suggestion.

The party wondered aloud whether she was a drow plant, and decided the drow would sooner die than use an elf. She bought her place among them with what she had overheard while serving Asha: word of the north passage. She went over the walkway with them, down through the webs and out into the Underdark, and it was Joy who suggested the party ask the duergar traders not merely to deny having seen them, but to send the drow down the wrong path.

She has since emerged as the practical one, and the only member of the party who wanted no part of going back into a town with a demon lord still standing in it. When the bandits were dead it was Joy who set the bodies alight without a flicker of hesitation, so the drow would find corpses in the party’s own shackles.

She is also, for a woman raised in a cage, oddly gentle with monsters: offered a badly camouflaged giant octopus in the shallows, she borrowed Stool’s spores and sent it feelings of danger until it inked the water and left, rather than let anyone draw a blade on it.
