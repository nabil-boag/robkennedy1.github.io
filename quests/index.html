Quests & Lore

Goals the party is chasing, and the mysteries and knowledge they have gathered along the way.

Quests

Completed

Escape Velkynvelve

Done. Jorlan kept his word and unlocked the slave pen at the changing of the guard. The party judged the armoury raid not worth their lives and left their gear behind, cut their way down through the giant spiders’ webs, outswam the ooze in the pool, and fled through the west passage while demons tore the outpost apart above them. Every prisoner got out.

Active

Eldeth's Belongings

Return Eldeth’s warhammer and shield to her family in Gauntlgrym, as she asked of Ilyrion before she was murdered.

The promise stands; the means of keeping it does not. Rather than risk a room full of drow for a pile of gear, the party abandoned the armoury raid on their way out — so Eldeth’s warhammer and shield are still sitting in Velkynvelve.

Active

A Murderer in Our Midst

Discover who strangled Eldeth Feldrun in her sleep and left her body posed as a sacrifice to the murder god Diinkarazan. The party has no proof, but suspicion has fallen on the unstable drow Sarith.

Active

Outrun Ilvara

No prisoners have ever broken out of Velkynvelve before, and Ilvara has taken it personally. She has sent an advance party of soldiers after the escapees and ridden out behind them with her commanders.

She does not know which of the three passages the party took. Keeping it that way is the whole game: the duergar trader Grimnar has been persuaded to point the drow down the wrong path, and everything the party does on the road — how fast they travel, what they leave behind them, who they talk to — will decide whether the pursuit closes or falls away.

Active

Take Stool Home

Help the myconid Stool return to his people in Neverlight Grove. In return, he has promised the party sanctuary among his kind.

Failed

Shuushar's Teachings

Help Shuushar the Awakened carry his teachings of peace and meditation back to his people, so that they too might avoid the madness that plagues the kuo-toa.

The party got him home. It made no difference: his own people called him a heretic, seized the party as sacrifices, and were tearing each other apart over two gods by the time Demogorgon rose out of the lake and flattened the town — with Shuushar in it.

He never got to teach them, and there is hardly anyone left to teach.

Failed

A Good Word for Grimnar

The duergar trader Grimnar agreed to lie to the drow about which way the party went. His price: that when they reached Sloobludop, Shuushar would put in a good word for his company with the kuo-toa and open up the trade there.

Shuushar is dead, Sloobludop has been destroyed, and there is no trade left there to open. The party held up their end of nothing at all — through no fault of their own, and with no way to tell Grimnar so.

Active

A Favour Owed

The goblins Yuck Yuck and Spider Bait guide travellers across the Silken Paths for two gold a head per day. The party had no money whatsoever, so Thibaud promised them a favour instead — unspecified, to be claimed whenever the goblins choose.

They were delighted with the arrangement. Favours are a powerful currency.

Active

The Lost Tomb of Kaine

Fergus Rumblefoot wants to go back for the treasure that killed his last party, and he wants this one to come with him. He has no money, no gear, and a demonstrated inability to reach the place without being eaten — but he insists he knows the way.

The party has not said no.

Lore & Mysteries

Lore

Grim Portents

On their final night in the pen, three of the party were visited by disturbing dreams. Rhys wandered lost through an endless maze of tunnels as dark shadows rose and reached for him. Ilyrion walked a hellscape where oily tentacles brushed against him and a great buzzing and howling rose from some huge, awful creature. Jebediah saw people clutching their heads and screaming in madness, their wounds bursting with maggots and insects. Rhys and Ilyrion woke at the very same instant. All three are left with the dread certainty that something far worse than the drow lurks in the depths of the Underdark.

What could the dreams mean?

Lore

Diinkarazan, the Murder God

An Underdark god of murder. Jebediah recognised the posed body of the murdered Eldeth Feldrun as an offering made to Diinkarazan — suggesting a worshipper of this dark god moves among the prisoners or the drow.

Lore

Blingdenstone

A deep gnome city in the Underdark, from which the drow drove the gnomes out. Jimjar revealed he is a spy from the surface city of Luruar, sent to investigate Blingdenstone’s current status.

Lore

The Prince of Nelrindenvane

The quaggoth Derendil claims to be an elven prince of Nelrindenvane, a kingdom in the High Forest. He says the evil wizard Terrestor transformed him into a quaggoth and seized his realm. Whether the tale is true, no one yet knows.

Lore

Flesh for the Faceless Lord

As the party dropped into the pool beneath Velkynvelve, every one of them heard the same words spoken directly into their minds:

Flesh for the faceless lord.

The grey ooze that rose out of the water was slow, mindless-seeming and easily outrun — and yet something had spoken. Nobody knows what a faceless lord is, or whether the ooze was its servant, its mouthpiece, or merely its leavings.

It is the second time the deep has spoken to them. See also Grim Portents — the dreams of tentacles, buzzing and madness that troubled Ilyrion, Rhys and Jebediah on their last night in the pen.

Lore

The Demons of the North Passage

The party’s escape was covered by an attack nobody expected. As they reached the cavern floor, alarm horns went up across Velkynvelve — not for the escaped prisoners, but for what was coming out of the north passage.

Two enormous decaying, vulture-like demons flew in pursued by four bloated, droning things like giant mosquitoes. They fought each other in the air above the outpost, smashing through rope bridges and slamming into the drow towers, while the garrison shouted and loosed crossbow bolts at all six.

Nothing about them looked natural — not even in the way that Underdark monsters look unnatural. One of the vulture-demons crashed wounded across the western exit and had to be killed before the party could get out.

The dreams warned of something worse than the drow. Something worse than the drow has now been seen.

Lore

Demogorgon Rises

When Ploopploopeen killed his daughter on the altar at Sloobludop, her blood called something up out of the Darklake.

Thirty feet and more of it stood out of the water: a single torso carrying two heads like enormous, furious baboons, oily tentacles thrashing, its eyes and throat burning red with what could only be described as bloodlust and madness. Every kuo-toa in the town — both sides of the civil war — dropped what they were doing and clutched their heads. Rhys looked at it too long and had to be dragged away babbling.

Jebediah recognised it from his religious training: Demogorgon, Prince of Demons, one of the most powerful beings in the multiverse.

It flattened Sloobludop while the party ran. This is what the dreams were warning about, and what the spectator meant when it said demons are rising in the underworld.

Lore

The Sea Mother and the Deep Father

Blibdoolpoolp, the Sea Mother, is the old goddess of the kuo-toa of Sloobludop — a scavenger goddess, to whom the discarded and the recovered are worthy offerings, the more personal the better. Her shrine was a nine-foot idol of carved wood wearing the severed head and claws of a giant crayfish, hung with shells and rotting fish. Vomiting at her feet is a sincere show of peace, which Jebediah happened to know and two of the party promptly demonstrated.

Leemooggoogoon, the Deep Father, is new. In two years a cult has grown up around him under Bloppblippodd, worshipping a thing of too many limbs and appendages, and drowning and cutting open sacrifices in ever greater numbers. Even the Sea Mother’s own archpriest admitted something in the lake had begun feeding on them.

Both congregations tore each other apart on the docks, and then Demogorgon rose and neither god’s followers were in any state to care.

Lore

Madness in the Deep

Something in the Underdark is getting into people’s heads, and the party is no longer merely hearing about it.

Sarith has complained of splitting pain since Velkynvelve. Shuushar said his whole people are prone to madness, and that it had been getting worse — his life of peace and meditation was an attempt to escape it. The Sea Mother’s own archpriest said the same of his congregation.

Then the party walked a narrow passage whose walls were riddled with holes, out of which came moaning, shrieking and gibbering in no language any of them knew. Kit was left paralysed for four minutes and Ilyrion unconscious for six, and both came out of it carrying something they hadn’t gone in with. The spectator on the Silken Paths spoke into their minds with thoughts tinged with madness — and warned them that demons are rising.

Looking at Demogorgon was worse still.
